Algae is a constant frustration in aquariums. However, the mechanisms by which algae develop seem to be commonly misunderstood. Therefore I have compiled a list of points, with citations, and an explanation for how to treat algae when it does occur. This is not intended to disparage any of the established methods; if they work for your tank, they work for your tank! More, it is intended to provide a scientifically grounded starting point for someone who has not been able to clear algae and is trying to find a direction.

This guide is limited to attached, non-siliceous eukaryotic micro-phytobenthos, or attached microalgae, or "ANSEMP". This is because the majority of algae fall under this umbrella and can be treated using the same approach. Thus, the below points do not include Diatoms, as they are not true algae and their cell wall is silica, Blue-Green Algae, as it is a cyanobacteria, not an algae, and Cladophora, as it must basically be treated like a weed due to being too biologically similar to plants.

1. Algae exist as dormant spores which are present at some level constantly. However, this is not a problem unless the spores are triggered to wake up.
2. Plants, when healthy, produce allelochemicals which prevent algae spores attaching to them, but have limited ability to affect established algal colonies.
3. Plants have a slower response to environmental changes than algae.
4. Algae feed on the same three "arms" as plants do (light, carbon, nutrients).
5. Algae tend to have less stable long-term nutrient reserves than plants.
6. Algae are much more efficient at photosynthesis, by biomass, than plants.
7. Algae are much more efficient at nutrient absorption, by biomass, than plants.
8. Once established, algae creates a negative feedback loop which outcompetes plants, as plants cannot remove "awake" algae.
9. Physically scraping algae off will only release it into the water column, which does not kill it.
10. Pllant stress (as plants release DOC when stressed, and stop producing allelochemicals, which allows algae to colonise their surfaces) is the primary cue for an algal bloom. 

And to clear up some common misconceptions:

1. You cannot eradicate algae from a tank completely, but you can reduce the occurrence of blooms.
2. Scraping algae off of a surface merely releases it into the water column; once it's in its adult form, even when removed from a surface, allelochemicals released by plants have very little effect.
3. Rebalancing your tank will merely prevent future outbreaks. Adult algae will not be killed and must be manually removed.
4. Algae can adapt to different photoperiods and a "siesta period" will not be a reliable fix.
5. "High light" does not cause algae alone, but it does reduce your margin for error, as if a trigger is introduced, algae will proliferate much faster.
6. "Nutrient imbalance" is a myth, or at least is misleading.
7. Fluctuating CO2 does not directly cause BBA. It causes plant stress, which attracts BBA.
8. All algae included in this guide generally respond to the same two ecological cues, those being plant stress causing DOCs and decreased allelochemical output.

#### Exceptions
As mentioned above, Diatoms, Blue-Green Algae, and Cladophora are not covered by the remarks above. However they are common, so I have tried to research them as well.

##### Diatoms
Diatoms are single-celled eukaryotes with a cell wall called a frustule made from silica. They are very beautiful, and in the Victorian era, were used in microscopy to create kaleidoscopic arrangements. Because diatoms consume free-floating silicates leached from raw sand, rocks, or new glass,[^1] they are naturally self-limiting and will die once available free silicates are entirely exhausted.

Diatoms are known for appearing in new tanks. I have never had a significant issue with them, and they tend to resolve after a couple of weeks, and I suggest leaving them alone to see if they disappear. However, if this approach does not work, manual removal is possible applying the same steps as those below. If your tap water is high in silicates, you may need to use remineralised RO to starve them. They can also be easily removed from surfaces using a soft-bristled brush or sponge.
