For this tutorial, we will be setting up a filterless shrimp and snail in a glass jar and silent cycling it. So, plants are very important to the health of the tank.

First, let's look at our ingredient checklist.

#### Steps
1. Find a glass (or other aquarium-safe material) container which can hold at least 1.5 gallons (5.7L) of water.
2. Fill it with about 5–8cm of substrate at the bottom.
3. Add some rocks or driftwood to add some areas for shade.
4. Put it somewhere that can hold 6–15kg long-term.
5. Set up the LED light by plugging it into the wall timer and setting it to turn on for 6–8 hours / day.
6. Gently fill with dechlorinated water (pour it over a bowl and/or siphon it in over an hour or so)
7. Plant any remaining plants.
8. Wait for any plant melt to be done.
9. Test water parameters to make sure there is no ammonia, nitrite, or nitrate, and that the pH, KH, and GH are suitable.
10. Add snails and wait for a couple of days.
11. Add food. Take sample of water 24h and 48h afterwards to test.
12. If snails are doing fine, test water parameters again.
13. Add shrimp when you're pretty sure they'll be fine by drip acclimating them over 2–3 hours.

*If at any point during this setup process your ammonia and nitrite are above 0, your plants are not taking up enough ammonia, meaning there is not enough plant mass being produced per day. Either add more plants, add faster-growing plants, or make the light brighter. Do not add shrimp until you're getting 0 for ammonia and nitrite.*

To maintain:
- Do only supplementary feedings occasionally (no more than once a week ideally)
- Add dechlorinated water when the water level drops too low
- Do a 10–20% water change every couple of weeks (even a couple of months)
- Test the water routinely to make sure that parameters aren't dropping

#### Plants
It's very important that you buy enough plants. Use this table and round up if you're unsure. Certain plants being more or less difficult is really just my opinion, everyone has different experiences, et cetera.

Go to a local fish shop or order online. You can easily spend $60/gallon on plants alone. You can also buy a plant pack/bundle online, which will be a lot cheaper. 

I recommend buying submersed plants to help skip the plant melt stage. I also recommend buying enough plants simultaneously. If you plant heavily from the start, the tank will balance much faster and you will not have as much of an issue with algae. You can keep plants in a bucket of water (in low light) for something like a week before they start really freaking out and dying.

#### Livestock
**Shrimp**
Go and get cherry shrimp! They're usually about $8 per shrimp, $6 if you can find them cheaply. Only get them when you're absolutely sure you're ready to add them though.

**Snails**
Bladder, Ramshorn, and Malaysian Trumpet snails are all considered pest snails and you can usually find them for very cheap, I paid fifty cents per snail and only bought maybe 5 snails, which quickly turned into 30. Don't buy too many snails at once. Ramshorn snails will get a bit sulky when they're moved, but after a few days they usually chill out a bit. Bladder snails reproduce VERY fast. I drip acclimate snails along with shrimp but I don't think this is super necessary.

**Daphnia**
If you get Daphnia/Moina, you will also need to get spirulina powder, and feed them something like 20mg of powder per gallon of water by dissolving the powder into the water. This turns the water green. I have not tried this but there is a lot of information online.

**Marimo moss balls**
If you still have room, you might want to get one of these. These are super cute and are actually a rare kind of algae. Literally just rinse it and put it in the tank. Then I guess you can rotate it occasionally.

#### Misc. notes
##### Hardscaping
If possible, using hardscaping and even substrate from an existing tank will help import some nitrifying bacteria over.

##### Testing water parameters
Some pet shops will let you bring in water for testing, or you can buy a testing kit, but they're $100.

##### Adjusting water parameters
Don't adjust water parameters unless you have to. Cherry shrimp are very adaptable, but they need time to adapt, and constantly adding chemicals or rebalancing the water will stress them.

##### Airstone
If you have an extra $30, a USB-powered air pump can be used by connecting airline tubing to a glass straw and stuffing a (clean) cigarette filter into the other end. Then suction cup it to the glass. If you have an extra wall timer, set this to only turn on when the light turns off. This really helps improve water circulation and gas exchange, and helps prevent the tank from crashing by increasing available oxygen in the water, which helps prevent a dissolved oxygen crash due to heterotrophic bacteria bloom (which happens when something rots) yadda yadda. But, it is not strictly *necessary* and can be quite intrusive depending on how loud an air pump you get and the bubbling noise.

Airline control valves and tubing will probably run you about $10. A lot of floating plants will die if the surface has too much agitation, so you might need to fiddle around with that.

##### Plant recommendations
I recommend getting feathery plants/stem plants with smaller leaves, and a lot of moss. Also, if you get a lot of light green plants, the blue/red/yellow line-bred shrimp will be very visible and look very bright.

##### Lighting
I would usually aim for 40 PPFD at substrate as a very high end for this kind of tank. You can do a bunch of maths to get a pretty good estimate but if you can't be bothered, you can just like... look at it, I think even with the lights on the tank should not feel "bright" at the bottom like it would if it were high tech. 

##### Easy water changes
Get a new cheap microwave lid cover which is larger than the top of the jar, flip it upside down and put it on top of the jar, open up a tiny hole in the bottom so water can slowly drip from it, and use a regular kitchen jug or something to pour water in. 

Or just use a bucket placed higher than the tank and a siphon. Use airline tubing to move water so you don't disturb anything.
